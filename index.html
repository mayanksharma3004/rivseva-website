The purification journey

Seven stages.
One glass of pure water.

01

Sponge

A spun sponge filter is the first line of defence, catching sand, silt and rust before anything else.

02

Sediment

The inline sediment filter removes the finer suspended dirt the sponge lets through.

03

Carbon

Activated carbon helps reduce chlorine, odour, colour and some organic impurities.

04

RO Membrane

A semi-permeable membrane reduces dissolved salts and many dissolved impurities. This is where the biggest change happens.

05

UF

A compact, half-size ultrafiltration cartridge holds back fine particles and many microorganisms.

06

Copper

A copper stage, found in many purifiers made for Indian homes.

07

Alkaline

A mineral stage that adds minerals back and can raise pH, for a balanced taste.

✓

Pure water.

Clear, fresh water for your family. Stages vary by purifier, so we check what your system has and what your water needs.

Frequently asked questions

Answers about RO care and our service.

Water quality varies from home to home, so good answers often start with "it depends". Here is what it depends on.

How often should an RO purifier be serviced?

There is no single schedule that suits every home. Many manufacturers suggest a routine check every three to six months, but the right interval depends on your water source (municipal supply, borewell or tanker), its dissolved-solids level, how much water you use each day and the purifier's design.

After checking your water and system, we suggest a schedule that fits your situation.

What factors determine RO service charges?

Charges depend on the type of work (routine service, repair, installation or relocation), any parts or consumables needed, your purifier's model and part compatibility, and the number of units.

We explain chargeable work and its cost before carrying it out, and proceed only with your approval.

When does an RO membrane need replacement?

Common signs include a noticeable drop in purified-water output, a change in taste, or a rise in output dissolved solids that cleaning and pre-filter replacement do not fix.

Membrane life varies widely with feed-water quality and how well the pre-filters are maintained, so we test before recommending a replacement rather than replacing on a fixed calendar.

Does every home need an RO purifier?

No. RO is most useful where water has high dissolved solids or specific contaminants. For water that is already low in dissolved solids, other technologies such as UV or UF may be more suitable. We recommend based on your water and needs, not a one-size-fits-all approach.

Can I book a service through WhatsApp?

Yes. Message us on +91 89550 58746, or use the booking form, which prepares a WhatsApp message with your details. Your visit is confirmed once our team replies to coordinate a time.

Do you provide RO maintenance for residential societies?

Yes. We offer society maintenance arrangements with a designated coordination point, organised records, preventive planning and periodic reporting. Coverage, response commitments and payment terms are agreed per contract. Learn about Society AMC.

Can RIVSEVA handle commercial RO systems?

We welcome service and maintenance enquiries from offices, institutions, restaurants and hospitality properties. Commercial systems vary a lot in size and design, so we assess each one first and will tell you clearly whether it falls within what we can support.

Which areas do you serve?

We currently serve Gurugram. Expansion across Delhi-NCR is planned. Share your locality when you enquire and we will confirm coverage.

What parts do you use?

We fit compatible components suited to your purifier, and we tell you what is being fitted before we fit it. Where a brand-specific part is needed and not available, we explain the options.

    Maintenance

    How often should you service your RO purifier?

    4 min readBy the RIVSEVA team

    It is one of the questions we hear most, and the honest answer is that it depends. Two identical purifiers in two different homes can need very different care. Here is what actually decides the schedule.

    Many manufacturers suggest a routine check every three to six months. Treat that as a starting point, then adjust it to your water and usage.

    What decides the right interval

    • Your water source. Borewell, tanker and municipal supply differ a lot in dissolved solids and sediment. Harder-working filters need attention sooner.
    • How much water you use. A large family that cooks with purified water puts far more water through the system than a couple who only drink it.
    • The purifier's design. Number of stages, membrane type and the manufacturer's recommendations all matter. Your manual is a useful guide.
    • How well earlier stages are maintained. A clogged sediment or carbon pre-filter makes everything after it work harder.

    Signs not to wait for the next scheduled visit

    • Output has slowed noticeably.
    • The taste or smell of the water has changed.
    • You see leaks, drips or damp patches.
    • The pump runs continuously or sounds different.

    Why replacing everything on a calendar is not ideal

    Changing every filter on a fixed date can waste money when parts still have life left, and can miss problems when water conditions are harsher than expected. A proper service checks performance first and replaces only what is genuinely needed.

    Our approach

    On a RIVSEVA visit, the technician inspects each stage, checks how the system is performing and explains what, if anything, needs replacing. You approve any chargeable work first. Over time we can suggest a schedule that fits your home.

    Water quality

    TDS explained: what the number really means

    5 min readBy the RIVSEVA team

    If a technician has ever dipped a small meter into your water and read out a number, that was probably TDS. It is useful, but it is often misunderstood.

    What TDS measures

    TDS stands for total dissolved solids: the amount of minerals, salts and other substances dissolved in water, usually given in milligrams per litre (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm). Calcium, magnesium, sodium, chlorides and bicarbonates all contribute.

    India's drinking water standard, BIS IS 10500:2012, sets an acceptable limit of 500 mg/L for TDS, with a permissible limit of 2,000 mg/L only where no alternative source is available.

    What a TDS reading cannot tell you

    A handheld TDS meter measures electrical conductivity. It does not detect bacteria, viruses, most pesticides or many specific contaminants. Water with a low TDS reading is not automatically safe, and water with a moderate reading is not automatically unsafe. For a full picture, a laboratory water test is the right tool.

    Is lower always better?

    No. Very low TDS water can taste flat, and some dissolved minerals are a normal part of drinking water. Many RO purifiers include a TDS or mineral adjuster so that output water is not stripped more than necessary. Where your purifier supports it, a technician can help set it sensibly for your feed water.

    How TDS helps during a service

    • Comparing the TDS of your feed water and purified water is a quick indicator of how well the RO membrane is working.
    • A purified-water reading creeping up towards the feed-water reading can be a sign the membrane needs attention.
    • Your feed-water TDS helps decide whether RO is the right technology for your home at all.

    This article is general information and not a substitute for laboratory water testing. Water quality varies between sources, sectors and seasons.

    Buying advice

    RO, UV or UF: which purifier suits your water?

    5 min readBy the RIVSEVA team

    Not every home needs the same purifier. The right technology depends mainly on what is in your water, so it is worth understanding what each one does before you buy or upgrade.

    RO (reverse osmosis)

    RO pushes water through a very fine membrane that reduces dissolved salts and many dissolved impurities. It is most useful where water has high TDS, such as some groundwater and tanker supplies. RO units produce reject water and usually need electricity and adequate water pressure.

    UV (ultraviolet)

    UV light inactivates bacteria and viruses. It does not reduce dissolved solids, and it works best on water that is already clear, because cloudy water can shield microbes from the light. UV is often combined with RO or used on its own where dissolved solids are already low.

    UF (ultrafiltration)

    UF uses a membrane with larger pores than RO. It removes suspended particles and many microorganisms, but it does not reduce dissolved salts. Some UF units work without electricity.

    A simple rule of thumb: if your water's dissolved solids are high, RO is often appropriate. If they are already low and the concern is microbial, UV or UF may be enough. Many homes use a combination.

    Questions to answer before choosing

    • What is your water source: municipal, borewell, tanker, or a mix?
    • What is the TDS of your feed water?
    • How much purified water does your household use each day?
    • Is your water pressure and power supply reliable?

    Water supply can vary from one sector or society to the next, so a neighbour's purifier is not always the right choice for you.

    Maintenance

    Signs your RO membrane needs attention

    4 min readBy the RIVSEVA team

    The RO membrane is the heart of your purifier, and usually one of its more significant replacement parts. That is exactly why it should be tested before it is replaced.

    Common signs

    • Purified water output has dropped noticeably.
    • The water tastes different, or salty.
    • The TDS of purified water has risen towards that of your feed water.
    • The purifier runs for much longer than it used to.

    Check these first

    Several of these symptoms can also come from other causes, such as a clogged sediment or carbon pre-filter, low inlet pressure, a weak pump or a faulty valve. A good technician rules these out before recommending a new membrane.

    What shortens membrane life

    • High dissolved solids and hardness, which can cause scaling.
    • Neglected pre-filters, which let sediment and chlorine reach the membrane.
    • Long periods without use, which can affect the membrane in some conditions.
    There is no universal lifespan for an RO membrane. It varies widely with water quality, usage and how well the earlier stages are maintained.

    Helping your membrane last

    Keep pre-filters maintained, use your purifier regularly, and have its performance checked periodically. Small, inexpensive steps early on often delay a bigger replacement.

    Saving water

    Making good use of RO reject water

    4 min readBy the RIVSEVA team

    Every RO purifier sends part of the incoming water down the drain. This reject water carries away the salts the membrane has removed. How much is rejected depends on the model, the membrane, the water pressure and your feed-water quality.

    Sensible ways to reuse it

    • Mopping floors and general cleaning.
    • Flushing toilets.
    • Washing vehicles, balconies and outdoor areas.
    • Pre-rinsing utensils before a final wash with regular water.

    Where to be careful

    • Not for drinking or cooking. Reject water is more concentrated than your feed water.
    • Plants, in moderation. High-salt water can harm some plants and build up in pot soil over time. Mix it with regular water or alternate.
    • Bathing and laundry. Very hard reject water may leave residue on skin, hair and fabrics.
    A simple setup, such as routing the drain line into a covered bucket or tank, makes reuse easy. Keep stored water covered and use it regularly.

    Reducing waste in the first place

    A well-maintained purifier with healthy pre-filters and correct pressure tends to run more efficiently. If your system seems to waste more water than before, it is worth having it checked.

    This article is general information. Reject-water quality depends on your feed water, so use judgement before reusing it.

    For RWAs & property managers

    What a good society RO AMC should include

    5 min readBy the RIVSEVA team

    For a residential society, RO servicing is rarely about one purifier. It is about hundreds of homes, many vendors and a steady stream of resident complaints. A well-written annual maintenance contract turns that into something organised. Here is what committees and property managers should look for.

    1. A clear scope

    The contract should say exactly which systems are covered (resident units, common-area or commercial systems), which visits are included, and what is excluded.

    2. Response commitments in writing

    Verbal promises are easy to make. Look for response times written into the agreement, with an escalation contact if they are missed.

    3. Transparent parts pricing

    Replacement parts are often where costs grow. Ask how parts are quoted, who approves them (the resident or the society), and whether chargeable work ever happens without approval.

    4. A single coordination point

    One designated contact for the management team, and a simple booking channel for residents, saves your office from becoming a complaints desk.

    5. Records and reporting

    Every visit should be recorded: unit, issue, work done, parts used and status. Periodic reports help the committee see what is happening and spot recurring problems.

    6. Preventive maintenance

    A planned cycle of preventive visits reduces breakdowns and spreads technician load evenly across the year.

    7. Sensible commercial terms

    Payment terms, renewal and exit terms should be clear and fair to both sides.

    A good AMC partner should be comfortable putting all of the above in writing. If they are not, that tells you something.
